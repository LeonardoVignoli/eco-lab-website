## Research and conservation of a Sardinian endemic

The Sardinian brook newt, *Euproctus platycephalus*, occurs only in Sardinia, where it inhabits mountain streams, springs and pools. The project combines distribution research, conservation in the wild and captive breeding to develop and evaluate reintroduction and reinforcement measures.

The programme involves the Department of Science at Roma Tre University, Fondazione Bioparco di Roma, the Sardinian regional government and the Fo.Re.S.T.A.S. agency. Ex situ conservation began in 2009; ISPRA issued a favourable opinion on the pilot reintroduction programme for 2019–2021 on 1 April 2019.

## Understanding distribution

Surveys in 2010–2015 documented 57 populations, compared with 14 used in earlier assessments. Combining field surveys, literature, interviews and observations helped update the distribution of this elusive species and identify gaps in existing knowledge.

{{< research-box title="Do more recorded populations mean recovery?" >}}
Not necessarily: more documented sites may reflect a wider search effort. Assessing trends also requires demographic data collected over time.

[Read the Oryx study](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0030605315001416).

## Captive breeding at Bioparco

The ex situ programme maintains groups from different source populations to represent the species’ genetic diversity. Captive-born animals are raised to a suitable release stage under husbandry and health-screening protocols. Aquaria include water cooling, filtration and aeration systems, with regular water-quality monitoring.

## Reintroduction in the Settefratelli Mountains

The release site was selected through an assessment of the species’ historical presence and field visits examining water availability, vegetation cover, threats and health risks. Genetic compatibility between captive animals and populations in the area is a central selection criterion.

On **1 October 2019**, **30 adults — 15 males and 15 females — were released**. Monitoring through summer 2020 recorded released animals and larvae, providing evidence of reproduction. Pandemic restrictions subsequently interrupted activities during part of 2020 and 2021.

In **2024**, **another 38 animals** were released at the Settefratelli site. Founder animals originally collected at **Perdasdefogu** and **Villagrande Strisaili** were also returned to their respective source sites that year.

{{< research-box title="Reintroduction, reinforcement and returning founders" >}}
Reintroduction aims to restore a population where the species occurred historically but disappeared. Reinforcement adds animals to an existing population. Returning founders means taking originally wild-caught animals back to their source site. Origin and genetic compatibility guide release destinations.
{{< /research-box >}}

## Recognising individuals and assessing outcomes

The monitoring protocol uses photographic identification based on pigmentation patterns, especially on the throat. Comparing photographs with the pre-release database helps recognise animals encountered again over time.

{{< figure src="/img/projects/euproctus/riconoscimento-fotografico.jpg" alt="Dark markings on the throat and underside of a Sardinian brook newt during photographic identification" caption="Throat markings are used for individual identification. Source: feasibility study, figure 8." >}}

The feasibility study sets out three years of monitoring, considering re-encounters, survival and reproduction. Larval observations are an important early result; assessing whether a viable population persists requires long-term observations.
